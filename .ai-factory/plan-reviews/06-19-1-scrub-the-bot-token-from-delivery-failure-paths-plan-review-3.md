### Positive Notes
- **The `__context__` hazard is correctly analyzed and correctly solved.** The plan's central claim — that `raise ... from None` suppresses `__cause__`/`__context__`-*display* but does NOT prevent CPython from re-populating `__context__` with the exception being handled at the moment of a `raise` *inside* an `except` block, and that assigning `err.__context__ = None` before `raise err` is overwritten by the `raise` — is accurate. Verified against CPython semantics: leaving the `except` block first clears the active exception (`sys.exc_info()` is restored to the pre-`try` state), so a `raise` at the loop-body level after the `try/except/else` establishes no implicit context and `__context__` is genuinely `None`. The code sample places `raise TelegramSendError(status)` at the correct indentation (loop-body level, outside the `try`), and the `else: continue` correctly routes the success path around it. This is the one subtle detail the task hinges on, and it is right.
- **No unbound-local hazard.** `status` is assigned in both `except` clauses; the only path that reaches `raise TelegramSendError(status)` is one where an `except` clause ran. The success path takes `else: continue` and never reads `status`. Sound.
